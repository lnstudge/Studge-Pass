Studge Pass: the password manager nobody can take away from you

A fully open-source, free password and passkey manager with no paid tiers. It is built to stay safe even if its own sync server is hacked, it helps people move from passwords to passkeys, and a foundation owns it, so no company can close it or start charging later.

The bet in one paragraph

In 2025 and 2026 the incumbents gave users reasons to distrust them. Dashlane ended its free plan. 1Password raised prices by up to 33%. Bitwarden almost doubled Premium and began shipping its app-store builds under a commercial licence. An ETH Zurich study showed that the "zero-knowledge" claims of Bitwarden, LastPass and Dashlane fail when the server is malicious. Meanwhile passkeys reached the mainstream (90% awareness, about 5 billion in use), and the FIDO exchange format finally lets people move them between managers. Studge Pass's bet: a manager that is free with every feature, cannot be relicensed, is designed for a hostile server, and leads users from passwords to passkeys can win people who distrust vendors (privacy-minded users, families and the KeePass/Bitwarden crowd), and it can do so without competing on price, because it has no price.

1 · The market in late 2026

Bitwarden

The open-source default. The free plan covers unlimited items and devices plus passkeys. Premium went from $10 to $19.80/yr in January 2026 (5 GB storage). Families costs $47.88/yr for 6 users. The paywall covers TOTP, vault health reports, emergency access and attachments.

Weak spots: ETH Zurich found 12 malicious-server attacks, 7 of which disclosed passwords (Bitwarden says they are fixed). There was the 2024 SDK licence scare. Since October 2026, store builds use a commercial licence and only the GitHub builds are GPLv3.

Proton Pass

Has the most generous free tier (unlimited logins, passkeys, 10 hide-my-email aliases, 2 vaults). Plus costs $35.88/yr, Family $59.88/yr and Unlimited $119.88/yr. TOTP, dark-web monitoring, attachments, item sharing and emergency access are paid.

Weak spots: clients are open source but the server is closed. It is younger, so it lacks travel mode and its autofill is less polished. Users report no CXP passkey import/export yet. The SSH agent is still on the roadmap. It is best value only inside the Proton ecosystem.

Dashlane

No free plan since 16 Sep 2025. Premium is about $59.88/yr, bundles Hotspot Shield VPN, dark-web monitoring and phishing alerts, and has no monthly billing. Friends & Family covers 10 people. It uses WebAuthn PRF for passwordless unlock.

Weak spots: closed source, and it is turning into an enterprise "credential protection" company. Emergency contacts were dropped along with the desktop app. ETH found 6 attacks, including a legacy-crypto downgrade that has since been removed.

1Password

Has the best UX in the category. There is no free tier. Individual rose to $47.88/yr and Families to $71.88/yr (+33%, from 27 Mar 2026). It offers Watchtower, Travel Mode (unique), an SSH agent, Agentic Autofill for AI browsers, and co-authored CXP.

Weak spots: closed source, cloud only, and the price keeps going up. One report says it classed the DEF CON 33 autofill-clickjacking bug as "informative" rather than patching it (disputed).

KeePassXC and the KeePass family

Fully free GPL software. Data lives in a local KDBX file you can sync any way you like. It has TOTP, an SSH agent, health checks with HIBP and passkeys in the browser extension (2.7.12 changed the backup flags in March 2026).

Weak spots: desktop only. Mobile depends on third-party apps (KeePassDX, Strongbox, KeePassium), and syncing and sharing are DIY with file-merge conflicts. The UX is for power users, and the FIDO exchange format is not supported.

Apple Passwords and Google Password Manager

Free, built in, and on by default for billions of people. Apple has TOTP, shared groups, password history (iOS 26) and CXP passkey export/import. Google offers family sharing (passwords only, not passkeys), passkeys with PRF, and CXP import/export rolling out during 2026.

Weak spots: they lock you into one ecosystem. Apple has no Android app and Google has no real iOS or desktop app. Google killed its Dark Web Report in Feb 2026. Neither offers self-hosting or vendor-independent recovery.

2 · The state of passkeys

Adoption

The FIDO Alliance's State of Passkeys 2026 report (World Passkey Day, May 2026) puts consumer awareness at 90%, up from 75%. About three-quarters of consumers have enabled at least one passkey and 49% enable them "whenever possible". FIDO estimates about 5 billion passkeys are in use. Most people therefore have a mixed vault: passkeys on some sites, passwords on the rest, and often both on the same site.

Portability: CXF and CXP

CXF (Credential Exchange Format) v1.0 became a FIDO Proposed Standard in August 2025, with errata published in March 2026. CXP (the transfer protocol, using HPKE over a Diffie-Hellman exchange) is still a working draft. In practice, transfers run through OS-mediated, on-device APIs. Bitwarden, 1Password and Dashlane support them on iOS 26, and Bitwarden also does on Android 14+. Passkeys that use signature counters cannot be transferred.

Where a third-party manager plugs in

  • iOS/macOS: AuthenticationServices credential-provider extension (third-party passkeys since iOS 17), plus ASCredentialExportManager and the matching import API in iOS/macOS 26.
  • Android 14+: Credential Manager provider service. A few OEMs still block third-party providers.
  • Windows 11: plugin passkey-manager API, live since the November 2025 update. 1Password and Bitwarden were the first partners.
  • Browsers: extensions still intercept navigator.credentials in the page. This is fragile and competes with native prompts, so use the OS provider wherever one exists.

PRF: unlocking the vault with a passkey

The WebAuthn PRF extension lets a passkey or security key derive a secret, which can unlock the vault without a master password. Bitwarden added this to its web vault and browser extensions (Chromium) in Nov 2025, and Dashlane uses PRF for passwordless accounts. Google Password Manager passkeys include PRF by default. The risk is lockout: if your only PRF passkey sits on a lost phone, you lose the vault, so recovery has to be designed in from day one.

4 · Gaps nobody fills well

Gap 1

Every feature free and fully open

KeePassXC is fully free but desktop only, with DIY sync. Bitwarden is open but paywalls TOTP, health reports and emergency access, and its store builds are now commercial. Proton's server is closed. No product gives you all of mobile, passkeys, sharing and recovery at $0 in a fully open stack.

Gap 2

Zero-knowledge you can verify

ETH Zurich showed that a hacked server can break vaults through unauthenticated metadata, key-substitution tricks in sharing and backward-compatible legacy crypto. Every product says "zero-knowledge". None publishes a malicious-server threat model with a public adversarial test suite.

Gap 3

Passkey migration help

Users now have mixed vaults. Nobody tells them which sites are passkey-ready, which accounts still have a weak password left behind a new passkey, or which single email account can reset 50 others.

Gap 4

Recovery without a vendor

Lose your master password and you lose everything, unless a company holds an escrow key. Social recovery (trusted people jointly restore access) exists in crypto wallets but in no mainstream password manager.

Gap 5

Storage freedom with good UX

KeePass gives freedom with clunky UX. Bitwarden gives UX tied to its own server (or a self-hosted copy). Nobody combines polished multi-device sync with "store the encrypted blobs anywhere".

Gap 6

Built for India and the next billion

Hundreds of millions of Indian users rely on Google Password Manager by default. No manager has Indian-language UIs, is tuned for low-end Android, or has templates for Aadhaar, PAN, UPI IDs, netbanking MPINs or DigiLocker.

5 · The concept: Studge Pass

Name. Studge Pass. "Pass" says plainly what the product does, and "Studge" makes it distinct and ties it to the founder's GitHub identity (lnstudge). Before a public launch, run a trademark search (India classes 9 and 42, EUIPO, USPTO) and check domains and app-store listings. Give it a visual identity clearly unlike Proton Pass, since both names end in "Pass".

Positioning. For people who no longer trust password-manager companies to stay free, open or honest, Studge Pass is the password and passkey manager that a foundation owns and no one can paywall, relicense or quietly weaken. Unlike Bitwarden, it has no paid tier and no commercial store build. Unlike Proton, everything is open, the server included. Unlike Apple and Google, it works on every platform and lets you leave with your passkeys.

Who switches first. (1) Bitwarden Free and Proton Free users who just hit a paywall or a price rise. (2) Ex-Dashlane free users. (3) KeePassXC users who want mobile, sync and sharing without the DIY. (4) Indian families managing parents' and children's accounts.

Five reasons people would switch

01

Free forever, by charter

Every feature is free with no tiers. The foundation's charter includes a "feature freedom pledge": nothing is ever paywalled, no ads, and no telemetry unless you opt in. Contributions use a DCO rather than a CLA, so nobody, including the foundation, can relicense the code as proprietary. This blocks the Bitwarden and Dashlane path at the root.

02

Safe even if our server is hacked

The server is treated as an attacker. Every byte is authenticated, the vault history is a signed hash chain, sharing keys sit in a public key-transparency log, and there are no legacy crypto modes. A public "evil server" test harness runs in CI so researchers can attack it the way ETH Zurich attacked the others.

03

Passkey Command Center

The dashboard gives one phishing-resistance score, a "blast radius" map of the email accounts that can reset everything else, and a queue of one-tap jobs: upgrade this site to a passkey, retire the leftover password behind that one, move your 6 passkeys out of Apple.

04

Recovery without us

Pick 3 guardians (family or friends who also use Studge Pass). Any 2 of them can help restore your account through Shamir secret sharing, without ever seeing your data. Emergency access and digital legacy (time-delayed handover) are included for free.

05

Your storage, your choice

Sync through the free Studge Pass Relay, a single self-hosted binary, or any folder: WebDAV, S3, Syncthing, Google Drive, iCloud Drive, Dropbox, or the LAN with no internet at all. This works because the server never has to be trusted. Offline-first everywhere.

Launch wedge, not a core differentiator: Hindi plus 10 other Indian-language UIs from v1, a 15 MB Android app that runs well on 3 GB RAM phones, and item templates for Aadhaar, PAN, UPI, netbanking, DigiLocker, passport and voter ID, plus a "family caretaker" vault for managing elderly parents' accounts.

Why not fork Bitwarden or extend KeePassXC?

Forking Bitwarden would inherit the protocol ETH Zurich attacked, along with its backward-compatibility debt and five large client codebases. Extending KeePassXC would inherit a file format and a desktop-first, volunteer-paced architecture that cannot sync or share well. Studge Pass should define a new protocol, then import from both (Bitwarden JSON, KDBX 4) and export to KDBX and CXF, so leaving Studge Pass is always one click.

7 · Security architecture

Design rule: the relay is an attacker that happens to store blobs. Every layer below answers one of the failure classes ETH Zurich found (unauthenticated metadata, key substitution in sharing, downgrade through legacy crypto), or the DEF CON 33 extension attack.

Login
OPAQUE asymmetric PAKE, so the server never receives a password-equivalent hash. The account key comes from Argon2id(master password) combined via HKDF with a 128-bit Secret Key generated on the device and stored in your recovery kit.A stolen server database cannot be brute-forced, even when the master password is weak.
Device keys
Unlock keys are wrapped by Secure Enclave, Android StrongBox or the TPM. A passkey with PRF or a FIDO2 security key can replace typing the master password.Biometric unlock is fast and the master password is rarely typed.
Item encryption
Per-item keys use XChaCha20-Poly1305. The AAD binds vault ID, item ID, field name and schema version.The server cannot swap fields or items between vaults, or replay old ciphertext into a new slot.
Vault integrity
Each change is a CRDT operation in a hash-chained log, signed by the device's Ed25519 key (ML-DSA hybrid later). Signed version vectors detect rollbacks and forks.The server cannot drop, reorder, roll back or forge vault state without detection.
Sharing
Vault keys are wrapped with HPKE using a hybrid of X25519 and ML-KEM-768. Public keys go into an append-only key-transparency log (a Merkle tree, audited by clients). Family members can compare safety codes.The server cannot slip in its own key when you share, and the scheme is ready for post-quantum attackers.
No legacy crypto
One cipher suite per protocol version. Migrations only go forward, and clients refuse to downgrade once an account has upgraded.Closes the Dashlane-style downgrade path.
Code
A single Rust core handles crypto, sync, item matching and import/export. It is shared through UniFFI (Swift, Kotlin) and WebAssembly (extension), with thin native UIs and zeroized memory.One audited implementation in place of five, written in a memory-safe language.
Browser extension
Fill prompts appear in UI the extension owns (toolbar popup, side panel or a native OS sheet), never in DOM the page controls. Origin matching is exact by default. Filling a cross-origin iframe needs confirmation.Removes the opacity-zero clickjacking class shown at DEF CON 33.
Supply chain
Reproducible builds, Sigstore-signed releases and an SBOM. Crypto and release changes need two maintainers. Anyone can check that store binaries match the source.The store build is the open build, with no commercial variant.
Assurance
Published threat model (malicious server, malicious sharee, compromised device). Protocol modelled in Tamarin or ProVerif. A public adversarial-server harness runs in CI. Two independent audits before 1.0, then yearly, all published. Public bug bounty.Trust that can be checked rather than promised.

9 · Licence, governance and funding

Licensing

  • Clients and Rust core: GPL-3.0-or-later, with a GPLv3 §7 additional permission for app-store distribution, granted from the first commit.
  • Relay server: AGPL-3.0-or-later, so hosted forks must publish their changes.
  • Protocol spec and vault format: CC BY 4.0 plus a royalty-free patent pledge, so others can build compatible clients.
  • Contributions: DCO sign-off, no CLA. Copyright stays spread across contributors, which makes a proprietary relicence practically impossible.
  • Trademark: held by the foundation under a Mozilla-style policy. Forks must rename, which protects users from malicious lookalike apps.

Governance

A non-profit foundation in the Netherlands (stichting) or Switzerland (Verein), or a fiscal sponsor at launch, with an India entity employing staff. Indian non-profits receiving foreign donations need FCRA registration, which is slow and restrictive, so keep donations outside India. The charter fixes the feature-freedom pledge, no ads, opt-in telemetry and an annual published audit. The board includes outside security researchers.

10 · Honest risks

Riskiest assumption

A project with no paywall can fund about 8 full-time engineers and yearly audits from donations, grants and support contracts for the 3+ years it takes to reach roughly 1M users. Every open-source manager that reached scale (Bitwarden) did it with paid tiers. KeePassXC survives on volunteers, but only on desktop. If funding falls short, the browser extensions and mobile providers decay first, and in a password manager that becomes a security problem within months.

Test it cheaply in 90 days: publish the threat model and protocol draft, apply for one NLnet grant, and run a founding-supporter pledge page. Proceed beyond the Rust core only with 2,000 pledged monthly supporters and one grant secured.
